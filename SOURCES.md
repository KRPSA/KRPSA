## 2026-10-15 IPSC in Motion reel

`reel.js` draws a 39-second explainer that plays as the first hero slide (and renders to video with `scripts/reel-render.mjs`). Everything is drawn in code: no third-party footage, photographs or logos other than the KRPSA crest. The pistol is a vector illustration made for the reel (see below); CZ and Shadow 2 are trademarks of Česká zbrojovka and are named only to identify the pistol. The stage (a 26-round long course over four shooting areas), its layout, shot times, splits and score are an illustrative demonstration, not a real match result or an approved IPSC course design. The speed ramps (slow motion on the swinger and the mover, fast forward on the runs between areas) are an editing device: the shot timer, the shot list and the hit factor always use stage time. The soundtrack is synthesised by `scripts/reel-audio.mjs` from the reel's cue sheet (no samples).

The development environment's network policy blocked direct downloads from ipsc.org, czub.cz, cz-usa.com and Wikipedia on 2026-10-15, so each fact below was cross-checked through web-search excerpts of the documents listed. The long-course entries were checked the same way later that day: the proxy also refused rules.uspsa.org, ipsc.cz, ipscaustria.at and dsf.dk. Rule numbers are those of the IPSC Handgun Competition Rules, January 2024 edition (“2024 Handgun Rules”): https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/IPSC-Handgun-Competition-Rules-Jan-2024-Edition-Final-27-Dec-2023.pdf , English/French printing https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/IPSC_Rules_Handgun_2024_EN_FR_VFinale2.pdf . Where an excerpt came only from a sister IPSC rule book or from the USPSA mirror, the entry says so. Re-check against the current IPSC Handgun Rules (January 2026 edition) before quoting numbers elsewhere.

- **DVC and history** — Diligentia, Vis, Celeritas (accuracy, power, speed); IPSC founded at the Columbia Conference, Columbia, Missouri, May 1976; 2026 is the 50th anniversary. https://www.ipsc.org/?p=1015 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Practical_Shooting_Confederation
- **Range commands** — “Make Ready”, “Are You Ready?” (no reply means ready; otherwise “Not ready”), “Standby” with the start signal 1–4 seconds later (rule 8.3.3), “If You Are Finished, Unload and Show Clear”, “If Clear, Hammer Down, Holster”, “Range Is Clear”. IPSC Handgun Competition Rules, January 2024: https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/IPSC-Handgun-Competition-Rules-Jan-2024-Edition-Final-27-Dec-2023.pdf
- **Scoring** — Comstock: hit factor = points ÷ time. Paper targets A 5 (Major and Minor), C 4 Major / 3 Minor, D 2 Major / 1 Minor; misses and no-shoot hits −10; scoring steel must fall to score and is worth 5. Same rule book; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Practical_Shooting_Confederation
- **Steel must fall** — rule 4.3.1.4: “Scoring metal targets must be shot and fall or overturn to score.” IPSC Poppers are calibrated to recognise power (rule 4.3.2.1, Appendix C1); metal plates are not. 2024 Handgun Rules (English/French printing); the same 4.3.1.4 wording is in the .22LR Handgun Rules, January 2025: https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/IPSC-.22LR-Handgun-Competition-Rules-Jan-2025-Edition-Final-29-Dec-2024.pdf
- **Production division** — only handguns on the IPSC Production list; Minor only, power factor ≥ 125; at most 15 rounds in a magazine at the start signal (Appendix D4); no optical sights. https://www.ipsc.org/handgun-production-division/ , list: https://www.ipsc.org/rules/divlistP.php . The limit applies to the magazine, so a round in the chamber is extra. The reel's shooter starts with 15 rounds in the gun, the slide locks back after shot 15, and the shooter reloads while moving to the last area.
- **Iron sights vs optic** — Production allows iron sights only; Production Optics uses Production-list handguns with a slide-mounted optical sight (Optics and Open also allow optics): https://www.ipsc.org/handgun-production-division/ , https://www.ipsc.org/handgun-production-optics-division/ . "Focus on the front sight" with iron sights and "focus on the target" with a red dot are standard marksmanship practice, shown as technique, not as an IPSC rule.
- **Targets** — IPSC cardboard target 46 × 58 cm (A zone 15 cm wide, C zone 30 cm wide, D zone to the edge), e.g. https://doubletapsports.com/product/ipsc-classic-targets-50/ and https://www.drummondshooting.ca/product/ipsc-zone-target/ . Zone heights are drawn in proportion, not as rule dimensions. IPSC Popper 85 cm tall with a round head and narrow base: https://www.ipscgear.com/product-page/ipsc-full-size-steel-popper-with-holes . Freestyle course solving is a general IPSC principle described in the same rule book; the 180° rule has its own entry below.
- **Long course** — rule 1.2.1.3: a long course must not require more than 32 rounds (short courses at most 12, medium at most 24: rules 1.2.1.1–1.2.1.2). Course design must also not require more than 9 scoring hits from any single location or view, nor let a competitor shoot every target from one location. 2024 Handgun Rules; the full long-course sentence was quoted from the Hong Kong edition, https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/IPSC-Handgun-Competition-Rules-Jan-2024-Edition-HKG.pdf , and the 32-round and 9-hit limits also appear in the IPSC Rules Quiz answers, https://www.ipsc.org/pdf/IPSC_Rules_Quiz_Answers.pdf . The reel's stage is 26 rounds, Comstock, over four shooting areas, with 130 points available (11 paper targets × 2 hits × 5, plus 4 steel × 5). It asks for 7 scoring hits from area A, 8 from area B, 5 from area C and 6 from area D, so no location exceeds 9. At 26 rounds it is above the 24-round medium-course cap, so it is a long course.
- **Moving targets and activators** — rule 4.1.6: only IPSC targets and mechanically or electrically operated devices may activate moving targets, so a Popper may release moving targets: in the reel P1 releases the swinger, and P2 releases both the drop turner and the mover. Rule 9.9, scoring of moving targets: a moving target that shows part of its A zone at rest, or keeps appearing and disappearing, always incurs failure-to-shoot-at and miss penalties. Any other moving target is a disappearing target (9.9.2) and incurs no such penalties, unless the competitor fails to activate it before or with the last shot. In the reel the drop turner is edge-on until released and shows its face once, and the mover (a paper target on a carriage crossing a rail) starts and ends hidden behind two barricades and is visible only in the gap between them, so both are disappearing targets. The swinger is held aside in full view before P1 releases it and only comes to rest behind its barrier, so it shows its A zone before activation and carries failure-to-shoot and miss penalties like any visible target; the shooter engages it twice while it swings. 2024 Handgun Rules; IPSC Rules Quiz answers, question 73 (rules 9.9.1 and 9.9.2); USPSA keeps the same structure as “Scoring of Activated Targets”: https://rules.uspsa.org/uspsa/section/9.9
- **Target arrays and stage props** — IPSC's Target Array Book (January 2019, the edition on ipsc.org, which the Handgun Rules cite for target arrays that will not be approved) says carnival target arrays such as “stars”, “plate racks”, “trees” and similar devices are NOT ACCEPTABLE, while up to 2 authorized plates presented above a sturdy common base may be acceptable (section 4.1): https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Target-Arrays-2019.pdf . This is why the reel uses two IPSC metal plates on one common base and a mover instead of a Texas Star or a plate rack. Metal plates of several sizes are approved IPSC metal targets (rule 4.3, Appendix C3), and a course must also include at least one scoring paper target or Popper. Ports: rule 2.2.7, “Windows and Ports”, must be at a height most competitors can reach; in the reel every area B target is shot through the port, with no-shoot NS2 overlapping T6 (2024 Handgun Rules). SSUSA describes swingers and drop turners as native to both IPSC and IDPA competition, and a Popper placed under a swinger's counterweight starts it swinging when it falls: https://www.ssusa.org/articles/2021/12/16/review-action-target-pt-swinger-pt-turn-swing . The mover is sourced as an IPSC moving target under rules 4.1.6 and 9.9 (entry above).
- **180° rule** — rule 10.5.2 disqualifies a competitor who lets the muzzle point uprange or past the default or specified safe angles of fire during a course of fire. The default safe angle is 90° in every direction, measured from the competitor facing directly downrange, which gives the 180°. 2024 Handgun Rules, rules 2.1.2 (Safe Angles of Fire) and 10.5.2. NROI describes it as a line parallel to the backstop that moves with the competitor: https://nroi.org/nroi-tips/nroi-tips-the-180/ . SSUSA names reloading while moving laterally as the most common way to break it, the beat the reel shows: https://www.ssusa.org/articles/2017/4/18/avoiding-the-180-dq
- **Unload and show clear** — rule 8.3.6, “If You Are Finished, Unload and Show Clear”: the competitor lowers the handgun and presents it to the Range Officer with the muzzle downrange, magazine removed, slide locked or held open and chamber empty. Rule 8.3.7, “If Clear, Hammer Down, Holster”: still pointing downrange, a self-loader's slide is released and the trigger pulled (8.3.7.1), then the gun is holstered (8.3.7.3); a gun that does not prove clear sends the commands back to 8.3.6 (8.3.7.4). IPSC's shooting course gives the order: remove the magazine first, then the chambered round, locking the slide back for inspection. The reel's unload beat follows that order: magazine out, slide locked back, chamber shown empty, slide forward and hammer down, holster, “Range Is Clear”. 2024 Handgun Rules; IPSC Combined Competition Rules (6 disciplines), January 2024: https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/IPSC-Combined-Competition-Rules-6-Disciplines-Jan-2024-Edition-Final-31-Jan-2024.pdf ; https://www.ipsc.org/ipsc-shooting-course/ , https://www.ipsc.org/so-handgun/
- **Hit factor and stage points** — rule 9.2.1, Comstock: time stops on the last shot, unlimited shots, a stipulated number of hits per target count for score. Rule 9.2.1.1: the highest-value stipulated hits minus penalties, divided by the total time to two decimals, gives the hit factor. The competitor with the highest hit factor receives the maximum points available for the course of fire and all others are ranked relative to that stage winner, so stage points = hit factor ÷ best hit factor × maximum stage points. The highest hit factor therefore wins the stage. The reel shows 120 ÷ 14.56 = 8.2418, of 130 points available. Comstock wording: 2024 Handgun Rules and the 2024 combined rules, https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024RulesCombined5Disciplies.pdf . The stage-points sentence was quoted from the IPSC Action Air Handgun Rules, January 2024, https://www.ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/IPSC-Action-Air-Handgun-Competition-Rules-Jan-2024-Edition-Final-27-Dec-2023.pdf , and USPSA rule 9.2.1.1.1, https://rules.uspsa.org/uspsa/section/9.2 ; worked example: https://ssusa.org/content/action-shooting-how-hit-factor-scoring-works
- **Pistol drawing** — the side-view pistol (a CZ Shadow 2, as used in Production) is traced from a left-side product photograph supplied by the site owner on 2026-10-15, mirrored to a right-side view and scaled to its ≈217 mm overall length. The reel does not name or describe the model; the gun appears only as the shooter's tool (the draw, the slide cycling, unloading). The first-person sight pictures are generic illustrations.
